Field notes — what the flock is actually doing

Separation

Never crowd the bird beside you. Strongest force of the three, and the reason a flock of ten thousand has no collisions. Falls off as 1/d².

Alignment

Match the average heading of everyone in range. This is what carries a turn across the whole roost faster than any single bird could fly it.

Cohesion

Drift toward where your neighbours are. Weakest pull of the three — enough to hold the roost together, never enough to collapse it into a knot.

And the fourth rule, which is why you are in trouble

Murmurations are partly mobbing behaviour — a defence starlings run against peregrines and sparrowhawks. A predator inside the flock gets swarmed, not evaded. Your survey drone reads as a predator. Every pulse you fire opens a corridor through the birds, and every corridor closes behind you within a second.
